# Project Datasets and instructions
The project is based on a genetic diversity dataset that was produced for the important African legume crop, [cowpea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowpea). You can download the dataset [here](https://cluster.hpcc.ucr.edu/~dkoenig/COURSE_DATA/project.tar.gz)

Cowpea is one of the most heat and drought tolerant crop species, and it is important that we understand how cowpea can survive in extreme climates across a gradient in West Africa. You will have environmental data and genetic data from a large number of cowpea varieties and your mission is to attempt to link genetic data to the environmental adaptation. At minimum, your analysis should analyze 5 environmental parameters.

## Analysis
1) An analysis of population structure in the dataset
2) A description of the relationship between population structure and your chosen environmental parameters
3) A GWAS study of the each of the 5 chosen parameters
4) An analysis of candidate genes that fall near the top peak of each of your parameters

For each of the above you should produce the relevant plots.

## Presentation
You will then produce a 5 minute presentation of your analysis including
1) A title slide
2) An introduction slide
3) 3 results slides
4) A conclusions slide
5) An acknowledgement slide

## Report
You will produce a markdown report of your findings. This report should include:
1) An introduction paragraph
2) A results paragraph. For each result clearly express the question you are asking, present a table or figure of results, and than describe your conclusion completely. This will require paragraphs not single sentences!
3) A summary of your results and your interpretations.

There is no specific length requirement for the report, but the more complete your report is at each step the better your grade will be! The text should probably be no less than three pages in 10 pt font (not including the figures).

## Extra Credit
You will receive extra credit of up to 10 points for meaningful additional analyses to those above. For example using maps to learn something about the data or analyzing an environmental parameter not provided in the original dataset.
